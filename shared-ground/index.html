Local History Installation · Mark Walhimer · 2026

Shared Ground

The same place. Different histories. An embodied local history platform for cities and local history museums.

Two histories in a room. When the people carrying them move apart, the archive diverges. When they move together, something new appears on the wall. Shared Ground is a platform for embodied collective memory.

The social geometry of bodies in space determines what history is visible.

Shared Ground

Its first instantiation is McDonough, Georgia, drawing on the Starr Photograph Collection, 1821 to the present.

AB
How It Works

The piece cannot exist with one person.

ONE PERSON

Solitary

A single person entering the room sees one thread of the archive: solitary, partial.

TWO PEOPLE

Diverging, converging

Their histories diverge on the same walls. As they approach each other, photographs appear that belong to both histories, where they overlap, contradict, or simply coexist.

THREE OR MORE

Every pair at once

Every pair forms its own relationship (A–B, A–C, B–C), and the archive responds to all of them at once. The more people in the room, the less any one person controls what surfaces.

What This Is Not
  • It is not a history lesson, and it does not resolve the histories it shows.
  • It does not assign photographs by identity. It responds only to movement and the relationships between bodies in space.
  • It is not tied to one place. It works in McDonough, in New Orleans after a flood, in Detroit after the plants closed — anywhere two communities remember the same ground differently. The photographs change. The logic stays the same.

First Archive

McDonough, Georgia.

The Starr Collection and Creek Nation records: more than 200 photographs, 1821 to the present, drawn from the City of McDonough historical collection.

The C.O. Polk Interactive Museum stands on land formerly inhabited by the Creek Nation and ceded by treaty in 1821. The land was later farmed by enslaved people, and unmarked graves of enslaved people lie in the local cemetery.

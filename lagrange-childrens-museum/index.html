LaGrange, Georgia Children's Museum

A downtown children's museum that celebrates Native American culture and builds a lifelong love of learning.

1. Description

The LaGrange Children's Museum is a planned museum in downtown LaGrange, Georgia, dedicated to Native American culture and to inspiring children to learn. Built around an extensive artifact collection, it aims to educate, to welcome everyone, and to draw visitors from LaGrange and across the state.

Its primary audience is children aged 3 to 12. It also serves adults with an interest in Native American history.

LocationDowntown LaGrange, Georgia
FocusNative American culture
AudienceChildren 3–12 and adults
Annual budgetAbout $1 million
Budget coversMaintenance, staff, programs, exhibits, outreach

2. Education and programming development

  • Age-appropriate curriculum aligned with school needs.
  • Interactive exhibits that invite hands-on discovery.
  • Storytelling that brings history and culture to life.
  • Hands-on workshops in crafts, music, dance, and storytelling.
  • School outreach and partnerships with local schools.
  • Special events throughout the year.
  • Online learning resources for classrooms and families.
  • Partnerships with Native American communities to ensure cultural accuracy.
  • Educational materials for teachers and visitors.
  • Evaluation using visitor feedback.

3. Interpretation and storytelling

  • Exhibit design developed with Museum Planning LLC.
  • Narrative development so every exhibit tells a story.
  • Multi-sensory experiences with lighting, sound, and touch.
  • Audiovisual presentations.
  • Staff and volunteer training.
  • Visitor engagement strategies throughout the galleries.
  • Accessibility and inclusion, including tactile elements, audio description, and captioning.
  • Rotating exhibitions to keep the museum fresh.
  • Live performances by Native American artists and storytellers.
  • Ongoing visitor feedback.

4. Visitor experience

  • Exhibit placement in a logical sequence of themed zones.
  • Wayfinding with clear, multilingual signage.
  • Timed entry or queuing for the most popular exhibits.
  • Accessibility, including ramps, elevators, and tactile and braille elements.
  • Interactive and VR/AR experiences.
  • Rest areas for families.
  • A circulation plan that keeps visitors moving comfortably.
  • Staff training in guest service.
  • Continuous improvement based on what visitors tell us.

A visit, imagined

A group of children steps through the doors. They walk past towering totem poles, duck into a ceremonial lodge, and lean over a diorama of a village alive with daily life. They press their painted hands onto a wall alongside hundreds of other visitors, make something of their own in the art workshop, and end the day gathered around a fire, listening to a story.

They leave with more than facts. They leave curious, and with a new respect for cultures different from their own. That is the museum's purpose.
